From Verilog to NMOS and PMOS

The RTL was synthesised with Yosys down to only four cell types: INV, NAND2, NOR2 and a D flip-flop. Every cell is a static-CMOS circuit of PMOS pull-ups and NMOS pull-downs. This page shows each cell, then walks through the Verilog line by line and names the hardware it becomes.

Counts come from gate_level/cell_counts.csv. The flip-flops split into 624 plain DFF (the storage array, no reset), 89 DFFR (registers that reset to 0) and 2 DFFS (empty and almost_empty, which reset to 1).

The four building blocks

Each cell as transistors

One rule builds every static-CMOS gate: the PMOS network pulls the output to VDD, the NMOS network pulls it to GND, and the two networks are duals. Where the NMOS transistors are in series, the PMOS transistors are in parallel, and the other way round. A PMOS conducts when its gate is 0 (the bubble); an NMOS conducts when its gate is 1.

DFF

Q ← D on rising C · 36 transistors

Six NAND3 gates (6 transistors each), the classic 7474 edge-triggered structure. While C = 0, g1 and g4 follow D. On the rising edge, g2 and g3 lock that value and set the output latch g5/g6. For DFFR the active-low reset drives CLR_n; for DFFS it drives PRE_n. A real standard-cell flip-flop uses transmission gates and needs about 24 transistors.

Real excerpt · gate_level/netlist_enc_w8.v

One parity bit, traced through the netlist

For 8-bit data the encoder places check bit 1 at codeword position 1. It covers positions 3, 5, 7, 9 and 11, which hold data bits 0, 1, 3, 4 and 6, so cw_o[1] = d0 ^ d1 ^ d3 ^ d4 ^ d6. Below is the gate cone Yosys actually produced, read from the output back to the inputs. The data bits themselves need no gates; the netlist simply wires them to their codeword positions.

Pairs such as NOR2(d1,d4) and NAND2(d1,d4) combine into an XOR of d1 and d4. Four such XOR stages chain the five data bits into one parity bit. The whole 8-bit encoder is 16 INV, 32 NAND2 and 29 NOR2, which is 276 transistors.

Where the 47,822 transistors go

Flip-flop numbers are exact. Encoder and decoder numbers come from synthesising those modules on their own, so the split of the remaining logic is approximate. Storage dominates: more than half of all transistors hold the 624 stored bits.

Each cell drives its output through a 10 ps delay that stands in for the RC delay of the node. Without it, the flip-flop feedback loops would race in zero simulated time.

Start with netlist_enc_w8.v (77 gates), then netlist_fifo_w8_d4.v. The 32 × 16 netlist has about 6,400 cell instances, too many to read by hand, but the same patterns repeat.

Library restricted to INV, NAND2, NOR2 and DFF for teaching. A foundry library adds AOI/OAI, XOR and smaller flip-flop cells, so a production netlist of the same RTL would use noticeably fewer transistors.
